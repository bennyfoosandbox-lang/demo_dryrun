1. NUS Libraries launches AI Sense Maker, Southeast Asia's first conversational AI research tool

NUS Libraries and NUS Information Technology unveiled AI Sense Maker (ASM), a web-based platform powered by OpenAI's GPT-4.1 that lets students and researchers query 150,000 digitised materials — including NUS's rare Southeast Asia collections dating to the 17th century and internal research papers — in plain language rather than exact keywords. University librarian Associate Professor Natalie Pang said the goal is to cut research-discovery time from an average of two weeks to around 30 minutes, using retrieval-augmented generation and an interactive knowledge graph to keep answers grounded in credible, cited sources rather than the open web. Demonstrated to media at NUS Central Library on 28 July, the tool officially launches in August for students, faculty and alumni, and NUS is billing it as the first deployment of its kind by a university in Southeast Asia. The launch drew strong, positive local tech and mainstream press coverage as a marker of NUS's applied-AI ambitions in higher education.

Sources: The Straits Times, techcoffeehouse.com

2. NUS engineers fuse four body signals into a single wearable patch with X-Sig

A team led by Assistant Professor Liu Yuxin at the NUS College of Design and Engineering's Department of Biomedical Engineering has developed X-Sig, a cross-modal skin sensor that stacks a conductive electrode and an ultrathin piezoelectric film to capture electrical signals (heart rhythm, muscle activity) and mechanical signals (pulse waves, muscle force) through one output channel instead of separate sensors. Worn on the wrist, it estimated blood pressure continuously and cufflessly with mean errors of just 0.27–0.33 mmHg against a standard cuff monitor, meeting the top IEEE accuracy grade for cuffless devices; on the forearm it classified ten hand gestures with 96.4% accuracy versus 72–83% for single-signal sensors. Published in Nature Sensors and publicised by NUS CDE on 28 July, the work points toward simpler, smaller, lower-power wearables for cardiovascular monitoring, prosthetic control and rehabilitation. It's the kind of hardware-innovation story that reinforces NUS engineering's reputation in medtech, following a string of similar sensor breakthroughs this month.

Sources: EurekAlert!, Medical Xpress

3. NUS's "lightbulb" reactor platform tackles hydrogen, plastic waste and methane in one design

Professor Yan Ning, Director of the NUS Centre for Hydrogen and Carbon Innovations, and his team showcased a chemical reactor that heats a thin metal filament — much like an incandescent lightbulb — to over 1,200°C to drive industrial chemical reactions while keeping the rest of the system cool and compact. NUS framed three previously separate studies as one versatile platform: near-complete ammonia-to-hydrogen conversion for hydrogen transport and storage, breakdown of common plastics such as polyethylene and polypropylene into reusable chemical building blocks, and conversion of methane into valuable manufacturing chemicals alongside hydrogen. Because the reactor runs on electricity and only needs to heat a small filament rather than a whole vessel, NUS says it is compatible with renewable power and could support a more energy-efficient, lower-carbon chemical industry. The story, published by the NUS newsroom on 28 July, was picked up by carbon-markets trade press as a notable decarbonisation development from Singapore.

Sources: NUS News, Carbon Pulse

4. National University Health System rolls out cluster-wide AI platform for pharmacy safety

The National University Health System (NUHS) — anchored by NUS's Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine, Faculty of Dentistry and Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health — introduced NCAIP, a cluster-wide AI platform bundling four tools (Admission/Discharge MedRecon, MedTriage and MedVerify) to automate medication reconciliation, triage and order verification for the roughly 1.2 million residents its pharmacy services support in western Singapore. NUHS says the tools already hit 88–100% accuracy in Phase 1 validation and could eventually save up to 850 pharmacist man-hours a week, worth about S$2 million annually, freeing pharmacists for complex clinical roles such as precision medicine and chronic-disease management as Singapore's population ages. Built on NUHS's existing ENDEAVOUR AI data-integration system, with human-in-the-loop review built in as a safeguard, the 28 July announcement adds to a run of NUS-linked health system stories this month showcasing applied AI in Singapore's public healthcare.

Sources: NUHS Newsroom

5. NUS Nursing's Zhou Wentao wins Singapore's top nursing honour, the President's Award for Nurses

Associate Professor Zhou Wentao, Deputy Head (Postgraduate Programmes) at the Alice Lee Centre for Nursing Studies, NUS Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine, received the President's Award for Nurses 2026 — Singapore's highest nursing accolade — from President Tharman Shanmugaratnam and Health Minister Ong Ye Kung. Over nearly three decades she has led three curriculum reforms of NUS's Master of Nursing programme, shortened the full-time track from 24 to 18 months without loosening standards, and in 2025 launched Singapore's first Doctor of Nursing Practice programme. Nationally she helped standardise advanced-practice-nurse training across all eight public hospitals and supported credentialling of over 300 nurse prescribers, while internationally she has served as treasurer of the International Council of Nurses' advanced-practice network. It's a prestige, human-interest story that adds to a string of NUS Medicine honours and appointments in recent weeks.

Sources: NUS Medicine (Alice Lee Centre for Nursing Studies), NUS News
