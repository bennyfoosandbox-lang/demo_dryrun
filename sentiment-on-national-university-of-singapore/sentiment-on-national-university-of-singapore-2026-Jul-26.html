2. NUS policy researchers warn Singapore faces a "social recession" of weakening ties alongside economic headwinds

Three researchers from the NUS Institute of Policy Studies at the Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy — Nicholas Thomas, Dr Wong Kang Li and Wilson Goh — argued in a CNA commentary that Singapore should watch for a "social recession" running in parallel with economic ones: weakening social ties, declining volunteering rates and thinner informal support networks that erode everyday resilience even when headline economic indicators hold up. They frame this as a risk that is harder to measure than GDP or unemployment but just as consequential for how households cope with shocks, and call for policy attention to community-level connectedness rather than economic metrics alone. The commentary extends NUS's Institute of Policy Studies' running contribution to Singapore's public-policy debate, adding a social-resilience angle to a week otherwise dominated by rankings and research news.

Sources: CNA, NUS News

3. NUS senior lecturer says Singapore's car-sharing boom leaves crash victims carrying the wait-time cost

Dr Liu Ziwen, a Senior Lecturer in the NUS Department of the Built Environment, wrote in Lianhe Zaobao that Singapore's fast-growing car-sharing fleets — now numbering in the thousands of vehicles across the island — need a stronger system for protecting third-party accident victims. Drawing on his own experience of being hit by a car-sharing vehicle and then facing a slow-moving third-party insurance claim even after fault was clearly established, he argued that the chain of driver, platform, insurer and repair shop leaves the not-at-fault third party bearing most of the delay. He noted that a 2025 CASE-LTA accreditation scheme for car-sharing operators followed 184 consumer complaints that year, but said it mainly addresses renters' disputes rather than third-party victims, and called on platforms to take a more active role in accident coordination — such as faster repair authorisation using dashcam evidence and public reporting of claims-processing times.

Sources: NUS News, Lianhe Zaobao (clipping, Chinese)
