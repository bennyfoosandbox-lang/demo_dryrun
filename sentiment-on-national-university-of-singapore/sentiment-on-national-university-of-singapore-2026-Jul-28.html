1. NUS engineers unveil a fully mechanical soft sensor that lets robots feel and react without electronics

A team from the NUS College of Design and Engineering has built ME-SOFS, a soft, 3D-printed force sensor that converts touch directly into fluid flow to trigger a robotic response — no batteries, circuits, or onboard computer required. The pea-sized sensor is built around a central pillar and five fluid-filled chambers, and a prototype glove packing five of them was 3D-printed as a single piece without manual assembly. The work was published in Science Advances on 8 July 2026, and targets soft robots operating underwater, inside the human body, or in other environments where conventional electronics are vulnerable or impractical. It's the kind of hardware-innovation story that reinforces NUS engineering's reputation for translational, real-world robotics research.

Sources: EurekAlert!, Tech Xplore, Interesting Engineering

2. Global study co-led with NUS Medicine finds Singapore's world-topping longevity comes with 11.2 "sick years"

A global analysis by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation, conducted with NUS's Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine, finds Singapore has the world's longest overall and healthy life expectancy — but the gap between the two is widening, with residents now spending an average 11.2 years in poor health. Drawing on Global Burden of Disease data, the study shows lifespan gains are outpacing "healthspan" gains worldwide, and NUS Medicine's Dr Marie Ng is quoted attributing Singapore's result to "deliberate long-term policy rather than any single cause." The finding, picked up internationally including by BBC Science Focus, adds nuance to Singapore's most-cited health ranking and puts an NUS-affiliated researcher at the center of the global conversation on healthy ageing.

Sources: SciTechDaily, BBC Science Focus

3. University of Exeter signs family-medicine research pact with NUS and NUHS

The University of Exeter has signed a memorandum of understanding with NUS and the National University Health System to collaborate on family medicine and primary care research. The three-way agreement will initially focus on healthy ageing, multimorbidity and digital innovation in healthcare, building on existing academic ties including a professor who holds joint appointments at both universities. It's a modest but concrete example of NUS's health system extending its international research network into UK primary-care expertise, adding to a steady stream of NUS Medicine partnership news this month.

Sources: University of Exeter News, Mirage News

4. Cancer survivor's NUS graduation story headlines Commencement 2026 human-interest coverage

Joshua Lee, diagnosed with Stage 3 testicular cancer at 24 midway through his NUS psychology degree, graduated with honours this Commencement season and is now speaking publicly about how the diagnosis reshaped his outlook. Both The Straits Times and NUS's own alumni magazine profiled his journey — including a psychology module on patients and healthcare he took specifically because of the experience — and his plans to marry his fiancée next year. It's a warm, widely-shared human-interest story in the same vein as other graduate profiles that have driven positive sentiment around NUS this month.

Sources: The Straits Times, NUS News, The Alumnus (NUS)

5. NUS engineering students join Stanford's GEDI programme, partnering with Meta and Venture Corporation

NUS College of Design and Engineering students completed their capstone showcase at Stanford University as new participants in the Stanford Global Engineering Design Innovation (GEDI) programme, joining for the first time in AY2025/2026.
