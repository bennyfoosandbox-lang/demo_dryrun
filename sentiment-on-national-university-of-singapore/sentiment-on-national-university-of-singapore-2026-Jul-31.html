1. AI-safety nonprofit FAR.AI opens its first office outside the US — anchored to NUS

FAR.AI, a US-based nonprofit focused on technical AI safety research, announced it is opening its first international office in Singapore — its first office outside the United States. The organization said it has been building ties with NUS for some time, and the new office deepens an ongoing research relationship with the NUS AI Institute through collaboration with Institute director Professor Mohan Kankanhalli, whose work on trustworthy and multimodal AI closely aligns with FAR.AI's mission. The move is a notable vote of confidence in NUS as a serious node in the global AI safety research map, not just a hub for AI adoption. The announcement went out via PR Newswire on 30 July 2026 and was picked up across Southeast Asian trade outlets.

Sources: PR Newswire, Vietnam Investment Review, NUS Newsroom (AI Institute background)

2. NUS Medicine signs MoU with Korea Regulatory Science Center to build a regional biohealth-regulation hub

NUS Medicine's Yong Loo Lin School and the Korea Regulatory Science Center (K-RSC) signed a Memorandum of Understanding at a ceremony at Post Tower in Seoul on 21 July 2026, agreeing to co-host academic conferences and regulatory science meetings, build joint certification courses and student exchanges, and share policy insights and case studies across ASEAN countries. Assoc Prof Seok Kwon Soga from NUS Medicine's Department of Pharmacology represented NUS alongside K-RSC Director-General Oh Jae-ho. The partnership aims to expand a pan-Asia regulatory-science network as biohealth regulatory expertise becomes an increasing bottleneck for the region's fast-growing biotech and medtech sectors. It extends NUS Medicine's recent run of cross-border regulatory and research tie-ups.

Sources: NUS Medicine, BioSpectrum Asia

3. NUS and Universiti Malaya co-host AI symposium spanning healthcare, robotics and more

NUS and Universiti Malaya (UM) jointly hosted an academic symposium showcasing AI research outcomes across healthcare, robotics and other fields, continuing a longstanding bilateral relationship that has also included joint golf tournaments and a 2025 Precision Health symposium. The event was reported by NUS's newsroom and reprinted by Singapore's Lianhe Zaobao on 30 July 2026, reflecting Singapore and Malaysia's push to deepen cross-border AI research collaboration in Southeast Asia. Sources: NUS Newsroom

4. Foundation Healthcare launches Singapore's first autonomous dental implant surgical robot with NUS Dentistry

Foundation Healthcare has opened the Foundation Oral Maxillofacial Surgery Centre, featuring Singapore's first autonomous dental implant surgical robot, developed by Beijing Yakebot Technology and the first dental surgical robot to hold both European CE certification and approval through China's NMPA Innovative Medical Device pathway. As part of the launch, Foundation Healthcare formed a strategic collaboration with the NUS Faculty of Dentistry to support research into robotic implant surgery and to provide education for clinicians and students. The robot combines 3D digital treatment planning with autonomous surgical execution under continuous surgeon supervision, positioning Singapore as an early adopter of frontier dental robotics in Asia. The tie-up gives NUS dental students and researchers direct access to a clinical-grade autonomous surgical platform.

Sources: BioSpectrum Asia, Dental Asia

5. CAVE@NUS immersive nursing-training hub gets fresh trade-press spotlight

A new trade-press feature spotlighted CAVE@NUS, the Classroom Automated Virtual Environment unveiled at NUS in June, as a model for how universities are embedding VR and AI into clinical training. Built with technology partner FXMedia, the 2,320-square-metre facility supports up to 350 learners at once through an immersive CAVE space and nine connected learning rooms, and underpins NUS Nursing's Virtual Escape Room Simulation-based Education (VERSE) programme, in which NUS and ITE nursing students work through realistic patient-care scenarios together. NUS Nursing head and VERSE principal investigator Professor Liaw Sok Ying said the initiative is among the first of its kind globally to build intra-professional education around this kind of technology. The renewed coverage frames CAVE@NUS as a template other institutions are watching as immersive learning infrastructure matures.

Sources: The National Law Review (EIN Presswire), NUS Newsroom (original feature)
